# 2026-08-16 — Candidates examined and REJECTED

Per the hard rules, a "no" is a valid and valuable answer. These were looked at in

## R1 — Sterile-processing / AEMP instrument-management software

**Rejected: market already served by established vendors.**

The obvious "insider builds software for their own department" idea. The category
is mature and the incumbents are already installed in Swiss hospitals — NEXUS /
EuroSDS is running at Spitäler Schaffhausen, and the Universitätsspital Zürich has
already rebuilt its AEMP.

Sources: [NEXUS AG](https://www.nexus-ag.de/aktuelles/artikel/digital-in-der-aemp-dokumentieren), [RecoTec](https://www.recotec-software.com/), [MCS MediClean Steril](https://cws-software.de/chargendokumentation-software/), [Klinik Einkauf — USZ](https://www.klinik-einkauf.de/blog/artikel/neue-agile-aemp-im-universitaetsspital-zuerich-a-42062)

Building enterprise hospital software as a side project against installed vendors
with procurement relationships is not a realistic solo path. Domain knowledge is
real here, but it is not enough to overcome the distribution gap.

## R2 — Build an AI documentation / ambient-scribe tool for practices

**Rejected: crowded, and newly high-regulation.**

The DACH market already has specialised providers with deep functionality and broad
practice-system integration — Ordicall, 321med, aaron.ai, Praxisconcierge,
telefonassistent.de — plus incumbents shipping it natively (Doctolib, medatixx
Health Hub) with integration advantages a newcomer cannot match. Entry pricing has
already settled at €49–199/month, meaning the price war has happened.

Decisively: **the EU AI Act phases in from August 2026 and classifies medical AI
applications as high-risk**, with strict requirements on human oversight,
transparency and documentation. That is a compliance burden aimed at well-funded
companies, arriving this month.

Sources: [Ordicall](https://www.ordicall.ai/blog/ki-telefonassistent-arztpraxis-vergleich-2026), [Doc Report AI](https://docreport.eu/blog/dsgvo-arztpraxis-software-datenschutz-leitfaden-ki), [abrechnungsstelle.com](https://abrechnungsstelle.com/spracherkennung/), [praxisANSATZ](https://praxisansatz.de/innovation/ki-in-der-arztpraxis/)

Note: *implementing* these tools for practices survives as Opportunity #3.
Building one does not.

## R3 — Productise the paper-trading bot (signals, subscriptions, or real broker API)

**Rejected on regulatory risk, not on technical merit.**

The existing bot is a genuine technical asset, but the moment it stops being a
personal tool and starts advising or trading for others in exchange for money, it
moves toward FINMA-regulated territory. In Switzerland, firms and individuals
active in the financial market require FINMA authorisation, and FINMA maintains a
public warning list of providers operating without it.

Sources: [FINMA — authorisation](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/authorisation-overview/), [FINMA — authorisation as a mark of quality](https://www.finma.ch/en/finma/activities/authorisation-licensing/), [descartes.swiss — FINMA warning list cases](https://descartes.swiss/de-ch/blog/finanzbetrug-schweiz-fallbeispiele-finma-warnliste/)

**Honest limitation:** I did not find a source addressing the specific line between
publishing educational trading content and providing regulated investment advice.
This is flagged as needing a lawyer, not as a settled finding. What is clear is
that the downside of getting it wrong (the FINMA warning list) is severe and
public, which is enough to keep it out of the top 5 without proper legal advice.

Keep the bot as a personal tool and a portfolio piece. It demonstrates real
technical skill for Opportunity #2 interviews.
