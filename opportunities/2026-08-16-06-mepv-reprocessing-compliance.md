# Opportunity #6 — MepV Reprocessing & Hygiene Compliance Support for Swiss Practices

Date found: 2026-08-16 (second scan of the day)
Status: New
Fit: The only candidate found so far that runs on the OR/sterile expertise itself
rather than on general clinical credibility

## Why this exists

The first scan proposed selling *digitalisation* to Swiss practices (Opportunity #3).
That was a real problem, but it was someone else's expertise. This is the same
customer, approached through the one thing an OR professional actually knows better
than the buyer: sterile processing.

## EVIDENCE (sourced)

- **It is legally mandatory.** Reusable medical devices, and non-sterile-supplied
  single-use devices, must be reprocessed according to the state of science and
  technology before use, following manufacturer specifications and hygiene
  requirements. — [Swissmedic](https://www.swissmedic.ch/swissmedic/en/home/medical-devices/reprocessing---maintenance/reprocessing.html)
- **The process is defined and auditable:** cleaning, disinfection, function check,
  packaging, sterilisation, transport and storage, using *validated* procedures. — [Swissmedic — Gute Praxis zur Aufbereitung von Medizinprodukten](https://www.swissmedic.ch/dam/swissmedic/en/dokumente/medizinprodukte/mep_urr/gute_praxis_zur_aufbereitungvonmedizinprodukten.pdf.download.pdf/gute_praxis_zur_aufbereitungvonmedizinprodukten.pdf)
- **Practices get inspected, and by a different body than hospitals.** Oversight of
  MepV compliance for hospitals sits with Swissmedic; **for practices it sits with the
  cantonal pharmacists (Kantonsapotheker)**. The cantonal pharmacist carries out
  inspections of reprocessing in medical and dental practices. — [Kantonsapothekervereinigung — KIGAP](https://www.kav-apc.ch/fileadmin/docs/public/kav/2_Leitlinien___Positionspapiere/KIGAP.pdf), [Kanton Thurgau — Pharmazeutischer Dienst](https://gesundheit.tg.ch/medizinisch-pharmazeutischer-dienst/pharmazeutischer-dienst.html/5563)
- **A formal inspection checklist exists** for reprocessing in practices operating
  small sterilisers and washer-disinfectors (RDG) under MepV — published via FMH
  guidelines, developed interdisciplinarily by KAV, SSO, KKA, Swissmedic and FMH. — [Guidelines Schweiz / FMH](https://www.guidelines.fmh.ch/index_detail.cfm?id=15EF7A1D403B46A9FE7E3B77D60AC05E), [FMH checklist PDF](https://www.guidelines.fmh.ch/downloads/15472918/Checkliste-Erlaeuterungen-de.pdf)
- **Buying external help is already the recommended path.** For new practices it is
  recommended to consult an external hygiene expert for needs analysis, hygiene plans,
  correct implementation of building installations, obtaining quotes and staff
  training. — [Swiss Cleanroom Community](https://www.swisscleanroomcommunity.ch/de/scc-community/community/blog?news=558)
- **The qualification is genuinely scarce, and gated by hospital employment.** The
  Swiss Fachkunde I course for technical sterilisation assistants (SGSV with
  H+ Bildung) runs 16 days over ~8 months — ~120 hours classroom, ~150 hours self-study,
  ~150 hours supervised practice — and **requires a practicum place or permanent
  employment in a ZSVA/AEMP in a hospital or surgical clinic** to enrol. — [H+ Bildung](https://www.hplus-bildung.ch/lehrgaenge-und-seminare/techn-sterilisationsassistent-in-fachkunde-i-ste-i), [berufsberatung.ch](https://www.berufsberatung.ch/dyn/show/2886?id=3834), [SGSV/SSSH](https://sssh.ch/de/)

## The strategic point

Read that last bullet again. The credential that qualifies someone to advise on this
**can only be obtained while employed inside a hospital sterile-processing
environment.** Practices, which need the knowledge, are structurally the last place
that can produce it.

That is a real moat, and it is **time-limited** — it depends on current hospital
employment. It is the only advantage found across both scans today that a
better-funded competitor cannot simply buy.

## Competitors (sourced)

Not a green field — this was checked rather than assumed:

- [krankenhaushygiene.ch — HYGIENE-TOOL®](https://krankenhaushygiene.ch/hygieneberatung) — independent Swiss hygiene consulting, explicitly covering hospitals, care centres, Spitex **and medical practices**
- [cleanprofis.ch](https://cleanprofis.ch/praxisreinigung-kantonsarzt-so-bestehst-du-jede-hygiene-kontrolle/) — practice cleaning, positioned around passing cantonal hygiene inspections (adjacent, not identical)
- Cantonal authorities publish their own checklists and guidance free of charge, which
  is the real competition: a practice can self-serve from the FMH/KIGAP documents.

## MY READ (assumption — not sourced)

- A practice that fails an inspection has a compliance problem with a deadline, which
  is a far sharper trigger to buy than "your admin is inefficient." Assumption about
  buying urgency — not measured.
- Cantonal fragmentation (26 cantons, each defining specifics) is probably an advantage
  for a local specialist and a disadvantage for a national vendor. Assumption.
- Dental practices may be a better beachhead than medical ones — instrument volumes are
  higher and the SSO is a named party in the guidance. **Unverified**; worth one search
  next scan.

## VALIDATOR — Demand evidence: **Some**

What is proven: the obligation is legal and specific, inspections genuinely happen
against a published checklist, a named cantonal body performs them, and using an
external expert is an explicitly recommended route.

What is **not** proven: that practices pay an independent specialist rather than
self-serving from free cantonal checklists or leaning on their steriliser vendor.
And no current data was found on how often practices actually fail.

**One trap avoided:** a 2012–2013 Bern cantonal pharmacy report shows 5 of 13 water
samples flagged for microbial contamination (~38.5%). That is a decade-old figure about
*water sampling*, not practice reprocessing failure rates. It is **not** valid evidence
for this opportunity and is recorded here only so a future scan doesn't mistake it for
support.

## STRATEGIST — plan

- **Offer:** A fixed-price "inspection-ready" review — walk the practice's reprocessing
  workflow against the official FMH/KIGAP checklist, write the gap list, fix the
  documentation, train the staff.
- **Target customer:** Small medical and dental practices in Ostschweiz running their
  own small sterilisers and RDGs. New practices being set up are the sharpest segment,
  since external expert involvement is already the recommended norm there.
- **Revenue:** Fixed fee per review, plus optional annual re-check before inspection
  cycles. Recurring by nature — the obligation doesn't expire.
- **Cost/effort to start:** The Fachkunde course is the real cost — 8 months alongside
  shift work, and it must be started *while still employed in a hospital*.
- **Biggest risk:** Advising on a regulated safety process carries liability. This needs
  professional indemnity insurance and a clear written scope before the first client,
  not after. Second risk: a conflict-of-interest question if advising practices that
  compete with or supply the employer — check the employment contract.

## BUILDER — first steps

1. **Answer the credential question first, because everything depends on it:** do you
   already hold Fachkunde I/II, and does your employer support enrolment? If yes, this
   opportunity is live. If no, it is an 8-month project before any revenue.
2. Download the FMH/KIGAP checklist and read it against what you already do daily. That
   tells you in an afternoon how big your real knowledge edge is.
3. Call the cantonal pharmacist's office for St. Gallen and ask, as a professional, how
   practices typically prepare for inspection and whether external support is common.
   This is free primary research and it directly answers the open validation question.
4. Ask 3 practices whether they have been inspected and what happened.
5. Price the Fachkunde course properly, and compare that 8-month commitment against
   Opportunity #2, which needs no credential at all.
6. Get an indemnity insurance quote **before** taking a paying client.
7. **Kill criteria:** if the cantonal office says practices self-serve from the free
   checklists and external support is rare, drop this — the free-checklist competition
   is the whole business case.
