If you do one thing this week

Have the same ten conversations once, and let them settle three of these five.

Opportunities #2 and #5 both hinge on one unknown: will people in your own profession pay you for something. Ten honest conversations with colleagues who have thought about leaving answers that for both at once — and the same conversations tell you where they buy their work gear. Meanwhile #1 costs nothing to start: the device vendors already in your OR are a warm target list nobody else has.

Everything below separates what a source actually said from what I inferred. Where demand couldn't be proven, it says so.

Freelance medical writing & MDR documentation

5.8Overall
Demand: Some–Strong, but only for qualified writers Real cash to enter

Advertised freelance rates in the German-speaking market run about €88/hour for medical writing and €110/hour for MDR work, and it's remote and schedulable around shifts. But every one of those data points describes the market for people who are already qualified. Entry is gated by real MDR and ISO training — months of study and the only meaningful cash outlay in this scan. Treat those rates as a ceiling, not a start.

Sterile-processing / AEMP instrument software

Incumbents already installed. The obvious insider play, but NEXUS is running at Spitäler Schaffhausen and the Universitätsspital Zürich has already rebuilt its AEMP. Domain knowledge is real; the distribution gap isn't bridgeable solo.

Building an AI scribe or ambient documentation tool

Crowded, and newly high-regulation. Entry pricing has already settled at €49–199/month, which means the price war happened without you. And the EU AI Act phases in from this month, classifying medical AI as high-risk. Note: implementing these tools for practices survives as #3 — building one doesn't.

Productising the paper-trading bot

Regulatory risk, not technical merit. The moment it advises others for money it heads toward FINMA-regulated territory, and the public warning list is a severe downside. I could not source where education ends and regulated advice begins — that needs a lawyer, not a scan. Keep the bot: it's a credible portfolio piece for #1's interviews.

Nursing staff leasing / temp arbitrage

Licensed activity. Personalverleih requires a licence and real capital. Parked without research — don't treat that one-line reasoning as verified.

Generic fitness / shift-worker wellness content

No unfair advantage. Fits your routine, but saturated and undifferentiated. Screened out before research.

What this scan can't tell you

  • No proof anyone will pay you personally. The macro evidence for #2 is strong — surveys, labour statistics, industry reporting. What I could not retrieve was direct community evidence: real people in forums asking for and paying for this help. That's why demand is rated “Some” and not “Strong,” and it's the first thing to go and find out.
  • The salary figures are German, not Swiss. The €46–56k and €55–80k bands for #1 come from German sources in euros. Swiss pay is materially higher. Read them as proof the role exists and pays professionally — not as an estimate of what you'd earn.
  • Some sources have skin in the game. The reporting on Swiss practices being digitally behind (#3) comes largely from vendors selling into that gap.
  • Nothing here is income. Finding an opportunity isn't making money, and every one of these can fail. Each write-up in the repo has explicit kill criteria for exactly that reason.
