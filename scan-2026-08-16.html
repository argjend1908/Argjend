What changed since run 1

Four real deltas. Everything else stands.

New · #4

MepV reprocessing compliance for practices. Legally mandatory, inspected by the cantonal pharmacist against a published checklist, and the qualifying credential is gated behind hospital employment. Scores 6.2 — mid-table, best moat found across both runs.

Upgrade · #2

Demand evidence rose from “Some” to “Some-to-Strong.” Real people asking the real question turned up — an active “Raus aus der Pflege” forum thread and named individual posts. The wording of those questions also reframed the product.

Rethink · #3

Right customer, wrong door. Approach practices with reprocessing compliance — where you have genuine standing and they face an inspection deadline — and sell digitalisation second. Score deliberately unchanged: the strategy improved, the economics didn't.

Rejected

4Rank

Found in run 2

MepV reprocessing & hygiene compliance for Swiss practices

6.2Overall
Demand: Some New Best moat found

Reprocessing reusable instruments is legally mandatory under MepV and must follow validated procedures. Practices — unlike hospitals — are inspected by the cantonal pharmacist, against a published FMH checklist. Here's the part that matters: the Swiss Fachkunde credential runs 16 days over eight months and requires employment in a hospital sterile-processing unit to enrol at all. The people who need this knowledge are structurally the last ones who can obtain it. That's a moat a competitor cannot buy — and it lasts only as long as your current job does.

Looked at and rejected

Logged so they don't come back as “new” next scan.

Corporate first-aid / BLS-AED training Run 2

Saturated and credential-gated. An obvious fit for a clinical background — and killed in a single search. Eight SRC-certified Swiss providers already serve companies on-site nationwide, with no visible underserved segment.

Sterile-processing / AEMP instrument software

Incumbents already installed. NEXUS is running at Spitäler Schaffhausen and the Universitätsspital Zürich has already rebuilt its AEMP. Domain knowledge is real; the distribution gap isn't bridgeable solo.

Building an AI scribe or ambient documentation tool

Crowded, and newly high-regulation. Entry pricing already settled at €49–199/month, which means the price war happened without you. And the EU AI Act phases in from this month, classifying medical AI as high-risk. Note: implementing these for practices survives as #3 — building one doesn't.

Productising the paper-trading bot

Regulatory risk, not technical merit. The moment it advises others for money it heads toward FINMA-regulated territory. I could not source where education ends and regulated advice begins — that needs a lawyer, not a scan. Keep the bot: it's a credible portfolio piece for #1's interviews.

Nursing staff leasing / temp arbitrage

Licensed activity. Personalverleih requires a licence and real capital. Parked without research — don't treat that one-line reasoning as verified.

Generic fitness / shift-worker wellness content

No unfair advantage. Fits your routine, but saturated and undifferentiated. Screened out before research.

What this scan can't tell you

  • Reddit is blocked to my crawler at the network level. The largest pool of candid career-change discussion simply cannot be searched with these tools — and no future scan will fix it. The forum evidence behind #2 is a partial substitute found elsewhere. Getting the real answer needs you browsing manually.
  • Free questions aren't proof of payment. Run 2 found people publicly asking #2's exact question. That proves the problem exists, not that anyone will pay for the answer — especially when a staffing company gives that answer away free.
  • One tempting statistic was deliberately thrown out. A Bern cantonal report shows ~38.5% of water samples flagged for contamination — but it's from 2012–13 and it's about water sampling, not practice reprocessing failures. It would have made #4 look better and it isn't valid evidence, so it's recorded in the repo as a trap rather than used.
  • The salary figures are German, not Swiss. The bands for #1 come from German sources in euros. Swiss pay is materially higher. Read them as proof the role exists and pays professionally — not as an estimate of what you'd earn.
  • Nothing here is income. Finding an opportunity isn't making money, and every one of these can fail. Each write-up in the repo has explicit kill criteria for exactly that reason.
